# What real TinyStories text looks like

Measured on 2,000 held-out validation documents, with a known-word set of 19,446 words taken from 100,000 training documents.

These bands are the reference the generated samples are judged against, so the coherence check tests *resemblance to the corpus* rather than anyone's opinion about good prose. Both edges matter: too much repetition is degenerate, too little is suspicious, because real children's stories repeat names and phrases deliberately.

## Why two kinds of statistic

The first four metrics vary document to document, so their band is the p5-p95 spread across 2,000 real documents.

The last two cannot be measured that way. Out-of-vocabulary words are rare enough that most single documents contain none, which is exactly what made the old `known_word_rate` metric degenerate: p5 and p95 both landed on 1.0 and the band was a point (ADR-022). They are **pooled** over every word in a group of 16 documents instead, and the band is the spread across 400 bootstrap resamples — the same statistic at the same sample size as the generated set it will judge.

`oov_plausibility` is the mean character-trigram log-probability of the words that are out of vocabulary. Vocabulary membership cannot tell an invented name from garbage, because both are unknown; character shape can. This is the metric Phase 5 needs when SFT starts producing names the corpus never used.
